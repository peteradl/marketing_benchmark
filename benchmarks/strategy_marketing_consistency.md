# Strategy Marketing Consistency Benchmark

## Purpose

This benchmark tests whether ADL marketing consistently expresses the 2026 business strategy. It evaluates strategic alignment, not post performance. Reach, engagement, clicks, conversions, and other performance metrics should be assessed separately and only when compatible analytics are available.

The benchmark is designed to answer one question:

> Does this marketing artifact help the audience understand ADL as a system designer that exists to build and support the Industrialized Construction industry in the United States?

## Source basis and authority

### Observed strategy facts

The following points are documented in [ADL Strategy 2026](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1907MF5amk6QGklsmTM6xhz4UiiCWvY8B/view):

- ADL creates value through three connected engines: the Engine, the Portfolio, and the Nonprofit.
- The Engine produces revenue today through systems design, transformation, and related work.
- The Portfolio builds enterprise value by creating ventures that fill gaps identified through the Engine's work.
- The Nonprofit strengthens the ecosystem through shared assets, standards, knowledge, data, designs, and convenings.
- ADL's flywheel moves from institutional owners to system design, gap identification, company transformation, venture creation, and a stronger industry.
- ADL's competitive moat includes privileged access to transformation problems, proprietary depth of understanding, and independence from selling a single Industrialized Construction solution.
- AI products convert Engine expertise into scalable leverage through software, knowledge, opportunity identification, and team formation.

The supporting [2026 ADL Operating Structure and Strategy Overview](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1lAp_22PsIMYJUsrVL_u5eP1ffrvjB0xpIWv0AH60yDs/edit) states that ADL exists to help build the Industrialized Construction industry and helps institutional owners design and deploy Industrialized Construction systems.

### User-directed marketing doctrine

For marketing purposes, the strategy is adapted into two explicit positioning requirements:

1. **Industry purpose:** ADL exists to help build and support the Industrialized Construction industry in the United States.
2. **Category identity:** ADL is a **system designer**, not a consulting firm. Advisory, research, transformation, program operation, and venture-building activities may be described, but they must support the system-design identity rather than replace it.

### Interpretation used by this benchmark

"System designer" means ADL helps organize the connected conditions required for Industrialized Construction to work at scale. Depending on the assignment, those conditions may include institutional demand, standards, manufacturers, suppliers, logistics, workforce, software, data, financing, policy, operating models, and implementation partners.

Use **Industrialized Construction** as the canonical term. Use **industrial construction** only when referring specifically to construction of industrial facilities.

## Core marketing doctrine

ADL marketing should leave audiences with the following understanding:

> ADL designs the systems that make Industrialized Construction deployable. It works with institutional owners and industry partners, converts field insight into shared industry assets and scalable products, and launches ventures to close critical market gaps. This strengthens the U.S. Industrialized Construction ecosystem while creating durable economic value.

Not every artifact must state the full doctrine. Every artifact should be compatible with it, and a healthy body of marketing should reinforce it repeatedly from different angles.

## Required strategic signals

### 1. Industry purpose

Content should connect ADL's work to a meaningful industry outcome, such as:

- accelerating Industrialized Construction adoption in the United States;
- improving cost, schedule, quality, resilience, housing delivery, or public-sector readiness;
- strengthening manufacturers, suppliers, workforce, standards, data, financing, or other ecosystem capabilities;
- helping institutional owners create repeatable demand and deployment systems;
- building shared knowledge or infrastructure that benefits the broader industry.

### 2. System-design identity

Content should show ADL working across relationships and system components, not merely delivering advice. Strong signals include:

- designing and deploying an integrated operating system;
- organizing owners, standards, supply chains, technology, workforce, logistics, and capital around a shared outcome;
- moving from diagnosis to implementation;
- reconfiguring existing assets and capabilities into a functioning system;
- identifying and closing market gaps;
- creating repeatable models, standards, platforms, tools, or ventures.

### 3. Integrated business model

Where relevant, content should connect the work to one or more of ADL's value engines:

| Engine | Marketing meaning | Useful evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The Engine | Designs systems, transforms organizations, and generates revenue today. | Institutional-owner work, deployment models, partnerships, implementation, field learning. |
| The Portfolio | Builds ventures that solve validated market gaps and create enterprise value. | Gap validation, EIR work, venture formation, commercialization, portfolio milestones. |
| The Nonprofit | Strengthens the shared ecosystem. | Standards, open assets, data, designs, research, roadmaps, conferences, and convenings. |

The artifact does not need to name an engine. It should make the strategic contribution recognizable.

### 4. Flywheel contribution

Strong marketing makes clear where the story sits in ADL's flywheel:

`Institutional owners -> system design -> gap identification -> company transformation -> venture creation -> stronger industry -> new institutional demand`

### 5. Independence and credibility

ADL should be presented as an independent system designer, not as a reseller or advocate for one predetermined product. Claims should distinguish:

- observed facts from interpretation;
- validated outcomes from projections;
- ADL's role from the roles of clients, partners, agencies, and portfolio companies;
- public information from confidential strategy, analytics, or client information.

## Positioning language

### Preferred patterns

- "ADL designs and deploys Industrialized Construction systems."
- "ADL helps institutional owners organize the demand, standards, supply chain, workforce, data, and operating models required for Industrialized Construction to work at scale."
- "The work revealed a market gap; ADL helped organize the partners and capabilities needed to close it."
- "This shared asset strengthens the broader Industrialized Construction ecosystem."
- "ADL moves from system insight to implementation, scalable tools, and new ventures."

### Use with care

These terms may accurately describe an activity, but should not define ADL's category by themselves:

- consulting;
- advisory services;
- strategy firm;
- program manager;
- accelerator operator;
- venture studio.

When one is necessary, connect it to the system-design role. For example:

> Weak: "ADL advised an owner on its construction strategy."

> Stronger: "ADL worked with the owner to design the demand model, standards, supplier network, and operating structure required to deploy Industrialized Construction."

### Off-strategy patterns

- Describing ADL primarily as a consultancy or a provider of reports and recommendations.
- Presenting disconnected services without showing how they strengthen a system.
- Implying that Industrialized Construction is a single product, factory, technology, or procurement shortcut.
- Presenting ADL as the seller of a predetermined construction solution.
- Treating a client, government award, event, or partnership as self-promotional news without explaining the industry value.
- Making unsubstantiated claims about impact, market leadership, funding, performance, or outcomes.
- Overstating ADL's role in work delivered with partners.

## Scoring rubric

Score each artifact on all six dimensions. Use only evidence visible in the artifact and its directly linked destination. Do not infer intent from internal knowledge that the audience cannot see.

| Dimension | Weight | Full-credit standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Industry purpose | 20 | Clearly connects the subject to building or strengthening the U.S. Industrialized Construction industry or a defined industry outcome. |
| System-design identity | 25 | Shows ADL integrating multiple system components and moving beyond advice toward deployment, implementation, or repeatable capability. |
| Strategic engine alignment | 15 | Makes the contribution to the Engine, Portfolio, or Nonprofit recognizable and consistent with the 2026 model. |
| Flywheel and ecosystem value | 15 | Shows how the work creates demand, reveals or closes gaps, transforms capabilities, launches ventures, or strengthens the ecosystem. |
| Evidence and role clarity | 15 | Uses supportable claims, names ADL's actual role, credits partners appropriately, and separates outcomes from projections. |
| Audience relevance and action | 10 | Gives the intended audience a clear reason to care and an appropriate next step or conclusion. |
| **Total** | **100** | |

### Dimension scoring

For each dimension, assign one of four levels:

- **Full credit:** Explicit, specific, and supported.
- **75% credit:** Clear but incomplete, generic, or weakly evidenced.
- **50% credit:** Neutral or only indirectly aligned; not contradictory.
- **0% credit:** Missing where essential, materially contradictory, or misleading.

Multiply the level by the dimension weight. Round the final score to the nearest whole number.

### Rating bands

| Score | Rating | Decision |
|---:|---|---|
| 85-100 | Strongly consistent | Publish from a strategy-consistency perspective after normal editorial and factual review. |
| 70-84 | Consistent | Publishable, but strengthen the weakest strategic signal when practical. |
| 55-69 | Needs revision | Revise positioning before publication. |
| 0-54 | Off strategy | Rework the artifact around ADL's purpose and system-design identity. |

## Override rules

The numeric score does not override a material strategic or factual risk.

- **Primary-consultant framing:** If the artifact defines ADL primarily as a consulting or advisory firm without a system-design frame, the maximum rating is **Needs revision**.
- **Single-solution framing:** If the artifact implies ADL sells or is structurally committed to one Industrialized Construction solution, the maximum rating is **Needs revision** unless the context clearly concerns a portfolio company or disclosed partner role.
- **Unsupported claims:** Unverified metrics, outcomes, leadership claims, or partner roles require an evidence hold regardless of score.
- **Confidential information:** Internal strategy, nonpublic client information, private analytics, or protected project details require a publication hold regardless of score.
- **Role inflation:** Failure to distinguish ADL's contribution from a partner's or client's contribution requires revision regardless of score.

## Application by surface

### LinkedIn post

Review the post text, creative, headline, and directly linked page. A short post may receive 50% rather than 0% on a strategic dimension when it is neutral and the dimension is not central to the post. It should still clearly reinforce at least one of these: industry purpose, system design, an engine, or a flywheel step.

### Website page

Apply a higher standard because the page has room to explain ADL's role. Service, About, Construction, program, and campaign pages should explicitly express the system-design identity and the industry outcome.

### Campaign or content series

Score each artifact and the series as a whole. A series may distribute the doctrine across multiple pieces, but the combined audience experience should cover purpose, system design, the integrated business model, and credible proof.

## Review output template

Use this structure when applying the benchmark:

1. **Artifact and observation window**
2. **Evidence reviewed**
3. **Strategy-consistency score and rating**
4. **Dimension scores**
5. **What reinforces the strategy**
6. **Contradictions, gaps, or evidence holds**
7. **Highest-impact revision**
8. **Suggested replacement language**, when useful

## Validation questions

Before treating version 1.0 as final, the marketing team should confirm:

- Whether "in the United States" is a permanent mission boundary or the current geographic priority.
- Whether **system designer** should always be capitalized in public copy.
- Whether the Engine, Portfolio, and Nonprofit names are approved for public use or should remain internal strategy language.
- Whether the Nonprofit is currently active, planned, or should be described only as a future-state component.
- Which public proof points are approved to substantiate the system-design identity.
