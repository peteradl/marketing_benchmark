# ADL Brand Guide v0.1

## Purpose

This guide defines the working visual and verbal identity for ADL Ventures. Version 0.1 resolves the most important inconsistencies in existing materials and provides a common standard for LinkedIn, the website, presentations, documents, campaigns, and partner-facing communications.

Version 0.1 is the approval draft. It should become version 1.0 after ADL confirms the logo masters, reverse-logo asset, typography licensing and availability, and public-use messaging.

## Brand foundation

### Core identity

ADL is a system designer that helps build and support the Industrialized Construction industry in the United States. ADL works across institutional demand, standards, manufacturers, suppliers, logistics, workforce, software, data, finance, policy, operating models, and implementation partnerships.

Marketing should make ADL feel:

- systems-minded;
- evidence-led;
- technically credible;
- outcome-oriented;
- collaborative;
- independent;
- optimistic without hype.

### Primary audience impression

An audience should come away understanding that ADL organizes the connected conditions required for Industrialized Construction to work at scale. ADL is not positioned as a conventional consulting firm, a report producer, or the seller of a single construction solution.

## Logo system

### Primary logo

Use the [full-color horizontal ADL Ventures lockup](https://drive.google.com/file/d/17_DUaOYz-N9x4iKhExIX56r1Qt8aM_uI/view) as the default logo. It provides the clearest recognition and should be used on websites, documents, presentation title areas, social graphics, event materials, and partner communications whenever space permits.

### Secondary logo

Use the [full-color stacked lockup](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1lvXIKKBWvg9bP3-z-JZlKhK6zELa0jQM/view) only when a vertical or near-square composition makes the horizontal lockup impractical.

### Symbol

Use the [peak symbol](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1zUyhiX6wXXACVCQXf4eEn1Nh1lSf55Jg/view) for avatars, favicons, small profile images, and applications where the full name would be unreadable. Do not use the symbol as the default signature on formal documents or unfamiliar-audience communications.

### Reverse use

On dark backgrounds, use only a verified official white or reverse master. Do not recolor the full-color logo manually. Until the reverse master is consolidated in the approved asset folder, place the full-color primary logo on a white or very light neutral field.

### Clear space

Maintain clear space on all sides equal to at least the height of the `VENTURES` letterforms in the horizontal lockup. No text, border, partner logo, or image detail should enter this area.

### Minimum size

- Horizontal lockup: at least 140 px wide digitally or 1.5 inches in print.
- Stacked lockup: at least 110 px wide digitally or 1.1 inches in print.
- Peak symbol: at least 24 px digitally or 0.25 inches in print.

When a logo becomes difficult to recognize, use a larger size regardless of these minimums.

### Prohibited treatments

Do not:

- stretch, compress, rotate, crop, outline, redraw, or rearrange the logo;
- change individual logo colors;
- add shadows, glows, gradients, or effects;
- place the logo over busy or low-contrast imagery;
- recreate the wordmark with a typeface;
- use a legacy or alternate mark simply because it is available in the archive;
- place multiple ADL logo variants in the same composition.

## Color system

### Core palette

| Role | Color | Hex | Primary use |
|---|---|---|---|
| ADL Charcoal | Dark gray | `#3F434D` | Primary text, headings, dark backgrounds. |
| ADL Blue | Slate blue | `#4F6D83` | Primary brand field, section bands, charts, secondary headings. |
| ADL Green | Green | `#79C29F` | Peak-mark color, supporting fields, ecosystem and positive-progress signals. |
| Accent Teal | Teal | `#119DBA` | Highlights, kickers, links, selected states, graphic emphasis. |
| Accent Blue | Bright blue | `#017CC4` | Charts, digital emphasis, secondary calls to action. |
| Steel Blue | Steel blue | `#6487A1` | Secondary data series, diagrams, subdued fields. |
| Muted Gray | Gray | `#727272` | Captions, metadata, secondary labels. |
| Utility Light Gray | Light gray | `#F2F3F5` | Background panels and table bands. |
| White | White | `#FFFFFF` | Primary light background and reverse text. |

### Functional colors

Use these only for status, risk, or data meaning. They are not general decoration:

| Meaning | Hex |
|---|---|
| Positive / on track | `#49B050` |
| Caution / attention | `#E7B416` |
| Risk / stop | `#CC3232` |

Always pair status colors with text, an icon, or a label. Never rely on color alone.

### Color proportions

For a typical ADL composition:

- 60-75% white or light neutral;
- 15-25% charcoal or ADL Blue;
- 5-15% green, teal, or blue accents;
- functional colors only where their meaning is required.

Avoid one-color compositions dominated by teal, green, or blue. The ADL palette works through contrast between restrained neutrals and selective accents.

### Accessible combinations

- Charcoal on white: approved for all text sizes.
- White on ADL Blue: approved for all normal text.
- Charcoal on ADL Green: approved for normal text.
- Muted Gray on white: approved for normal text, though Charcoal is preferred for long passages.
- White on Accent Blue `#017CC4`: use for large or bold text; it sits just below the normal-text contrast threshold.
- White on Accent Teal or ADL Green: do not use for normal text. Use Charcoal instead.

All final digital and print artifacts should meet WCAG AA contrast: 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text and essential graphics.

## Typography

### Primary typefaces

- **Libre Franklin:** headings, titles, section labels, pull quotes, and short display text.
- **Roboto:** body copy, captions, tables, labels, metadata, footnotes, and long-form reading.

### Fallback stack

When the primary fonts are unavailable:

`Libre Franklin, Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`

`Roboto, Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif`

Do not substitute decorative, condensed, serif, handwritten, or novelty fonts without approval.

### Hierarchy

- H1: Libre Franklin Bold; concise and outcome-led.
- H2: Libre Franklin Semibold.
- H3: Libre Franklin Semibold or Medium.
- Body: Roboto Regular.
- Emphasis: Roboto Medium or Bold; avoid excessive all caps or italics.
- Captions and metadata: Roboto Regular or Medium.

Use sentence case for headings. All caps may be used sparingly for short labels and kickers. Do not use negative letter spacing.

### Readability

- Keep body copy left aligned in most applications.
- Avoid fully justified text.
- Use comfortable line spacing, approximately 1.35-1.55 for body copy.
- Keep digital body text at 16 px or larger when practical.
- Use no more than three clear hierarchy levels in a single social graphic.

## Layout and composition

ADL layouts should feel structured, direct, and information-rich without becoming crowded.

- Use a visible grid and consistent alignment.
- Prefer white space and clear grouping over decorative containers.
- Use full-width bands, rules, and restrained panels to organize information.
- Do not place cards inside other cards.
- Use one dominant message per social graphic or presentation slide.
- Keep page and slide titles compact enough to match their container.
- Use accents to direct attention, not to decorate empty space.
- Keep the logo in a consistent corner or signature area across a series.

## Imagery and graphic language

### Photography

Prefer images that show the real system: facilities, manufacturing, components, institutional owners, workforce, logistics, installation, data, or collaboration in context.

Avoid:

- generic corporate handshakes and meeting-room stock photography;
- purely atmospheric construction imagery;
- dark, blurred, or heavily filtered images that hide the subject;
- imagery that presents one product as the entirety of Industrialized Construction;
- photos without confirmed usage rights.

### Diagrams and data graphics

ADL's strongest graphic language is systems-oriented. Use flows, maps, timelines, matrices, process diagrams, and evidence-led charts when they clarify relationships.

- Use the core palette consistently across a graphic.
- Label data directly when possible.
- Include a source and observation date for external data.
- Distinguish observed results from forecasts or illustrative values.
- Keep chart decoration minimal.

### Icons

Use a consistent icon family within each artifact. Prefer simple line or solid icons with comparable stroke weight. Do not mix unrelated illustration styles.

## Voice and language

### Voice principles

- **Systems-minded:** explain relationships, dependencies, and how parts work together.
- **Evidence-led:** support material claims and name uncertainty.
- **Plainspoken:** prefer direct language over jargon and slogans.
- **Outcome-oriented:** focus on what changes for owners, partners, and the industry.
- **Collaborative:** credit clients, agencies, partners, founders, and communities accurately.
- **Confident, not inflated:** show expertise through specificity rather than superlatives.

### Preferred language

- Industrialized Construction
- system design / system designer
- institutional owners
- industry partners
- design and deploy
- strengthen the ecosystem
- identify and close market gaps
- repeatable capability
- standards, supply chain, workforce, data, software, finance, and operating models

### Language to use with care

- consulting;
- advisory;
- innovation firm;
- venture studio;
- accelerator operator;
- transformation;
- disruptive;
- best-in-class;
- world-leading.

These terms may describe an activity, but should not replace ADL's system-designer identity or appear as unsupported claims.

### Editorial conventions

- Use `ADL` after the first appropriate reference to `ADL Ventures`.
- Capitalize `Industrialized Construction` when naming the field or ADL's strategic domain.
- Use the serial comma.
- Prefer active voice.
- Keep sentences and paragraphs short enough for the surface.
- Define specialized acronyms at first use.
- Use numerals for measurements, dates, percentages, and quantified outcomes.
- Verify names, titles, agencies, organizations, funding amounts, dates, and metrics before publication.

## Partners, programs, and proof

- Describe ADL's actual role precisely.
- Credit partners in proportion to their contribution.
- Do not imply endorsement by a government agency, client, or partner.
- Obtain approval before using partner logos, client marks, quotes, or private project details.
- Separate awarded funding from ADL revenue and total program value.
- Separate validated outcomes from targets, projections, and illustrative examples.
- Do not expose confidential strategy, analytics, procurement information, or protected project data.

## Surface guidance

### LinkedIn

- Use a recognizable ADL visual signal in company-page graphics: approved logo, type hierarchy, and core palette.
- Design for mobile viewing; keep essential text large and limited.
- Lead with an industry insight, outcome, or system problem rather than an announcement about ADL.
- Use images and diagrams that show the actual subject.
- Tag partners only when the relationship and contribution are accurate.
- Put detailed sourcing in the post, graphic, or linked page as appropriate.

### Website

- Use the primary horizontal logo in the global header when space permits.
- Apply Libre Franklin to headings and Roboto to body copy.
- Maintain consistent button, link, spacing, and image treatments.
- Make the system-designer identity and Industrialized Construction purpose visible early on priority pages.
- Meet WCAG AA for contrast, alt text, keyboard navigation, and focus states.

### Presentations and documents

- Use approved templates as a starting point, then apply this guide where legacy templates conflict.
- Use Charcoal title bands, white content fields, and selective green, teal, or blue accents.
- Keep titles conclusion-led.
- Use source notes on charts and quantitative claims.
- Mark confidential or restricted materials clearly and consistently.
